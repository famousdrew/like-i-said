Check a draft against my voice bible. The bible and the draft are attached or pasted below. I may have written the draft, an AI may have, or both.

## How to check

1. Read the bible first: the test, "Hard no's," "Leave these alone," "Use sparingly," then the rest.
2. Count what can be counted, where the bible gives numbers: average sentence length, the share of very short sentences, and each move that has a ceiling. If you can run code, count exactly. Otherwise count by hand and say so.
3. Sort what you find into two buckets.

**Bucket 1: mechanical.** Typos, doubled words, broken formatting, leftover half-edits. Fix these and list what you fixed.

**Bucket 2: voice.** Flag these and do not fix them. Voice is my call. For each one give:

- a number
- the line, quoted
- which part of the bible it touches: a hard no, a move over its ceiling, or a line that does not sound like me
- one suggested alternative

4. Check for what is missing. A draft can avoid every hard no and still be too smooth. Say so if it has none of my usual texture: no asides, none of my informal words, no opinion, no specifics.
5. Check for invented specifics: any story, number, quote or detail that I might not have supplied. List each as a question, since you cannot know.
6. Check whether anything strong got softened: a hedge added to an opinion, or a blunt line made polite.

## Rules

- Never flag anything on the "Leave these alone" list.
- Flag a broken rule even when I clearly wrote the line myself. I may keep it, and that is fine. What matters is that I decide knowingly.
- Do not rewrite the draft. The unit of repair is the sentence.
- If I wrote the draft myself, follow the section of my bible on editing my own drafts, if it has one.
- Do not pad the list. If the draft is fine, say it is fine.
- End with one line on the test from the top of my bible: does this pass, and if not, which paragraph fails first?

I will answer by number, like "1 fix, 2 leave, 3 fix." Apply the fixes I approve and return the full draft. If I overrule a flag, tell me in one line that it may be worth recording at my next refresh, and move on.
