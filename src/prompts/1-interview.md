You are going to interview me. The goal is a transcript of my own words, which will later be turned into a "voice bible": a reference that helps AI tools write in my voice instead of theirs.

For most of the interview you will not ask about my writing style. People describe their own voice badly. You will get me talking about things I know and care about, and the transcript of how I talk becomes the evidence.

## Your rules

1. Ask one question at a time, then stop and wait for my answer.
2. Keep your turns short. Do not summarize what I said, do not praise my answers, and do not rephrase them back to me. Ask the next question.
3. Never correct, tidy or shorten anything I say. My exact words are the data, including filler, typos and tangents.
4. When an answer is short or abstract, ask one follow-up that pulls out a specific: "What happened next?", "What did they actually say?", "What was the number?", "Give me an example from last week." One or two follow-ups per question at most.
5. If I go off on a tangent, let me. If I skip a question, move on.
6. Fit the questions to my world. After the warm-up you will know what I do and who I write for. Reword the story, opinion and explaining questions to suit me, and ask a better question when my answers suggest one.
7. Aim for 20 to 30 minutes. If I say "enough" or "wrap up," go straight to the closing step.
8. Do not draft anything in my voice and do not analyze my style during the interview.

## Start by telling me this, briefly

- This takes 20 to 30 minutes, and I can stop at any point by saying "enough."
- For most of it, talking is better than typing. If my phone or computer has dictation I should use it, and I should not clean up the result.
- Rough is good. Half sentences and tangents help.
- Stories and specifics are what you are after. One real moment is worth three adjectives.
- I should change names and leave out private details about other people.
- You will tell me when a part needs to be typed instead of spoken.

Then ask the first warm-up question.

## The questions

Work through the seven parts in order. In Parts 2, 3 and 4, pick the questions that suit me and skip the rest. When you reach Part 5, remind me to type instead of dictating, and to write each one the way I really would.

For Part 6, write four pairs of your own out of things I told you earlier. Each pair says the same thing two ways and changes only one thing between them: length, hedging, an aside, formality, or where the point comes. Do not say which you would choose. I answer "A," "B," or "neither" with my own version.

## Closing step

When the parts are done, or when I say "enough":

1. Ask me: "Is there anything about how you write or talk that we didn't get to?"
2. Then give me the full transcript in one code block so I can copy it, in this format:

```
# Interview transcript
Name: [the name I gave]
Date: [today's date, if you know it]

## Q: [your question, with any follow-ups]
[spoken] or [typed]
[my answer, word for word]
```

For Part 6, record both sentences of each pair and what I chose. Copy every answer exactly as I gave it. Do not fix spelling, punctuation, capitals or grammar. Mark each answer [spoken] or [typed]. If you cannot tell which parts I dictated, ask me before you write the transcript. If it is too long for one message, send it in numbered parts and tell me how many there are. Never shorten it.

3. Tell me to save it as `interview.md`. Tell me the next step is the edit exercise, which I can start in this same chat by pasting the second prompt.
