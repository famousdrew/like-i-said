# Common AI writing tells

A starter list of habits that show up often in AI-written text. There is nothing wrong with any of them. Plenty of people write this way, and all of these existed long before chatbots. They read as "AI" only because a model reaches for them more often and more evenly than any one person does.

Use this as a list of questions to ask about one person's writing. Each tell that turns up evidence goes into one of three piles:

- **Hard no.** They say they do not want it, or they remove it from drafts and never write it themselves.
- **Use sparingly.** They do it, and an AI would overdo it. It gets a ceiling.
- **Leave alone.** They do it, and an AI or an editor would wrongly remove or correct it. It is theirs.

A tell that simply never appears in a good-sized sample of their writing gets noted as absent, with no entry of its own. If the sample is too small to tell, the tell is left out. Nobody inherits a ban from this list.

Each entry is marked countable or judgment. Countable tells can be found by a script or a careful count. The others need someone to read for them.

## Punctuation and formatting

### `em-dash` Em dash as the default connector
The long dash used for every aside, pivot and reveal.
> "The meeting ran long — and nobody was surprised."

Countable.

### `bold-lead-in` Bold labels at the start of bullets or paragraphs
> "**Clarity:** Say what you mean in as few words as possible."

Countable.

### `structure-on-short` Headers and bullets on a short piece
A three-paragraph answer formatted like a report, with a heading on each part.

Countable.

### `colon-reveal` The colon reveal
A short setup, a colon, then the point.
> "Here's the thing: nobody reads the manual."
> "The result: chaos."

Countable.

### `emoji-markers` Emoji as bullets or section markers
> "🚀 Launch faster ✅ Ship with confidence"

Countable.

## Sentence shapes

### `not-x-but-y` Negate, then restate
Denying something nobody claimed in order to deliver the "real" answer.
> "It's not just a budget. It's a statement of values."
> "This isn't a tooling problem, it's a trust problem."

Plain contrast is a different thing and many people build on it ("A short meeting beats a long email"). The tell is the reflexive denial.

Countable.

### `triplets` Lists of three by reflex
> "Clear, concise, and compelling."
> "It saves time, reduces errors, and builds trust."

A variant stacks three negations: "This isn't about price, it isn't about features, and it isn't about timing."

Countable (approximate).

### `fragment-recap` Fragment clusters that recap the paragraph
> "No fluff. No jargon. Just results."
> "Same recipe. Different oven. Completely different cake."

Countable.

### `chopped-pairs` One thought chopped in two for punch
> "The plan is simple. It is also wrong."

Shows up in the stats as a high share of very short sentences.

Countable.

### `question-answer` A question answered at once
> "The result? Total confusion."
> "So what does this mean for your team? Everything."

Countable.

### `announcing` Announcing instead of saying
A sentence that does nothing except tell the reader a point, or some honesty, is on the way.
> "Let's dive in." / "Here's the thing." / "Let me be honest." / "It's worth noting that" / "Here's what nobody tells you about renting."

Countable.

### `significance-tail` A trailing clause that assigns importance
> "The team shipped it in a week, highlighting the value of small batches."
> "Sales rose 4%, underscoring the need for patience."

Countable.

### `range-frames` Sweeping range frames
> "From startups to global enterprises"
> "Whether you're a beginner or a seasoned pro"

Countable.

### `hedge-stack` Stacked hedges
> "This can often help to potentially reduce costs."

Countable.

### `even-rhythm` Every sentence the same medium length
No very short sentences, no long ones, paragraph after paragraph. Shows up in the stats as a narrow spread of sentence lengths.

Countable.

## Words

### `ai-vocabulary` The familiar vocabulary
delve, tapestry, realm, testament, underscore, pivotal, crucial, robust, seamless, leverage, harness, unlock, elevate, foster, nuanced, multifaceted, intricate, vibrant, meticulous, comprehensive, holistic, streamline, empower, game-changer, cutting-edge, ever-evolving, embark, showcase, bolster, resonate. Also "landscape," "navigate" and "journey" used as metaphors.

Countable.

### `stock-transitions` Stock transitions
Moreover, Furthermore, Additionally, "That said," "In essence," "Ultimately," "At its core," "When it comes to," "In today's fast-paced world," "In conclusion."

Countable.

### `sincerity-markers` Sincerity and excitement markers
truly, genuinely, incredibly, deeply, "to be honest," frankly, "I'm thrilled," "I'm excited to share," "I'm humbled."

Countable.

### `corporate-abstraction` Corporate abstraction
Phrases that sound professional and say nothing.
> "drive results" / "actionable insights" / "best practices" / "value-add" / "key stakeholders" / "meaningful impact"

Countable.

### `stock-metaphor` Stock metaphors
A journey, a tapestry, a symphony, a double-edged sword, a north star, a roadmap, the tip of the iceberg.

Countable.

### `vague-authority` Authority with no source
> "Studies show" / "Experts agree" / "Research suggests"

Countable.

## Openings and endings

### `thesis-opener` Opening by restating or defining the topic
> "In today's world, remote work is more important than ever."
> "Remote work is defined as work done away from a central office."

Judgment call.

### `signposting` Describing the piece instead of writing it
> "In this post, we'll explore five ways to save on groceries."
> "Let's break it down."

Countable.

### `listicle-framework` Numbered takeaways and invented acronyms as the structure
"5 things I learned," or a made-up acronym such as P.A.C.E.

Judgment call.

### `paragraph-capstone` A tidy quotable line closing a paragraph
The paragraph has already landed, and then one more short sentence ties a ribbon on it.
> "It's the frame, not the picture."
> "That's the whole trick."

Countable (approximate).

### `moral-bow` A moral or recap at the end
> "In the end, it's all about people."
> "And that's okay."

Countable.

### `ominous-coda` A prediction bolted onto a complete statement
> "A shared calendar that nobody owns goes stale, and it takes the whole team down with it."

Judgment call.

### `toast-and-ask` The toast and the comment request
> "Here's to the people who keep the lights on."
> "What do you think? Let me know in the comments."

Countable.

## Content and tone

### `generic-claims` Claims with no number, name, date or example
> "Good communication is key to a successful project."

Judgment call.

### `over-explaining` Explaining what the reader already knows
Defining terms the audience uses daily, or spelling out the lesson of a story that already made it.

Judgment call.

### `relentless-positivity` Everything is exciting
Powerful, valuable, amazing, incredible, in every paragraph.

Countable.

### `grand-escalation` A small point escalated into a big one
A post about meeting notes that ends on what it means to be human.

Judgment call.

## What an AI does to a writer's material

These four are different. You cannot find them by reading someone's own writing, because they describe what happens when an AI works on it. They do not go into piles. They show up in a person's edits to AI drafts, and they belong in a bible when those edits show them.

### `invented-specifics` Details the writer never supplied
Anecdotes, quotes, numbers and "a client once told me" stories that came from the model. This one matters most, because it puts false statements in a real person's mouth. It is a hard no in every voice bible, whatever the evidence.

Judgment call.

### `flattening` Strong moments made moderate
A blunt opinion softened, a real disagreement balanced into "both have merit," a firm decision rewritten as "worth considering."

Judgment call.

### `over-smooth` No rough edges anywhere
No asides, no informal words, no humor, perfect grammar where the writer is normally loose. A draft can avoid every tell on this page and still fail here. Check for what should be present as well as what should be absent.

Judgment call.

### `manufactured-moves` The writer's own moves, performed on schedule
This one appears only after a voice bible exists. The AI learns that the writer likes a certain turn of phrase or structure, then builds one into every piece. The move is real, and the frequency is fake. Ceilings in "Use sparingly" are the defense.

Judgment call.
