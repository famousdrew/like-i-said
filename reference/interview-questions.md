# Interview questions

These questions get you talking about things you care about, so there is a record of how you sound when you are not trying to sound like anything. Almost none of them ask about your writing style. People are poor witnesses to their own voice, and the answers to "describe your style" come out as adjectives nobody can use.

## How to answer

- **Talk for Parts 2 to 4.** Use dictation on your phone or computer. Speaking is faster and less guarded than typing.
- **Rough is good.** Half sentences, tangents and filler are all useful. Do not fix anything afterward.
- **Stories and specifics.** One real moment is worth three adjectives. Give the number, the name, what was actually said.
- **Follow a better question.** If one of these makes you think of something else you would rather talk about, answer that.
- **Skip freely.** You do not need all of them. Twenty to thirty minutes is plenty.
- **Protect other people.** Change names, and leave out anything private about someone else.

## Part 1: Warm-up

1. What do you write, and who reads it?
2. Where does AI come into your writing right now, and what bothers you about what it gives back?
3. What name should your voice bible use for you?

## Part 2: Stories (pick three)

4. How did you end up doing what you do? The real version, not the bio version.
5. Tell me about a specific person who changed how you work or think. What did they actually do or say?
6. Describe a time when something went wrong and you were part of the reason.
7. What is a moment from the last month that you have already retold to someone? Tell it to me the same way.
8. Tell me about a habit or instinct of yours and where it comes from.

## Part 3: Opinions (pick three)

9. What is a piece of advice everyone in your world repeats that you think is wrong?
10. What is your hot take, the one that might annoy people you respect?
11. What do people get wrong about what you do?
12. What is something you changed your mind about, and what changed it?
13. What is a small thing that annoys you far more than it should?

## Part 4: Explaining (pick two)

14. Pick something you know cold. Explain it to a smart friend who is new to it.
15. Someone has just inherited a mess in your area. Where do they start on Monday?
16. Walk me through how you actually do a task you do often, including the parts that are not in any manual.

## Part 5: Typed (do all three, and type them)

These capture how you write when you write: capitals, punctuation, greetings and sign-offs.

17. A text to a friend cancelling plans tonight.
18. A short email to someone at work saying no to a request.
19. The first three or four sentences of a post or article about your answer to question 9 or 10.

## Part 6: This or that

Reacting is easier than describing. Each pair says the same thing two ways. Pick the one that sounds more like you, or say "neither" and write it your way.

If an AI is interviewing you, it writes four pairs out of things you said earlier, and these four are only a fallback.

20. "It broke on Tuesday. We fixed it by lunch." or "The problem came up on Tuesday, and by lunchtime the team had worked out a fix."
21. "I think this is probably the wrong call." or "This is the wrong call."
22. "The numbers were bad (really bad), so we stopped." or "The numbers were bad, so we stopped."
23. "Three things went wrong, and the first was mine." or "It was raining when I got to the office, which should have been a warning."

## Part 7: Preferences (quick answers)

These are the only questions about style. Your answers get recorded as things you said you want, kept separate from the evidence of what you do.

24. When you read something written by AI, what gives it away for you? What makes you wince?
25. Are there words or phrases you would never use?
26. Is there anything people tease you for saying or writing all the time?
27. Do you have punctuation or formatting habits you know about? Dashes, ellipses, all lowercase, lots of parentheses, emoji.
28. Do you swear in writing? Does it depend on who is reading?
29. How does your writing change between a note to your boss, a public post and a message to a friend?
30. What is something you never want to sound like?
31. When an AI edits something you wrote yourself, what should it leave alone? Do you want small fixes or a fresh version?
32. If English is not your first language: do you want to keep the turns of phrase that come from your first language, or have them smoothed out?
